MOTION NOTES — 12 practical editing directions

These are illustrative starting points. Supply your own assets and measured timestamps.

1. Kinetic Typography
=====================

Use: A punchline, a promise, or a short phrase that deserves emphasis.
Build: Remotion / a local text animation, composited over the approved footage.
Check: Keep the phrase readable. Align emphasis with the spoken word rather than moving every word constantly.

PROMPT
Animate “SHOW UP / DO THE WORK” over a charcoal background for 6 seconds. Use bold cream condensed type and one warm orange accent. 0–0.7s: reveal SHOW UP upward through a horizontal mask with 60ms letter stagger. 0.7–2.0s: hold. 2.0–2.35s: DO THE WORK snaps into the lower line, landing at 104% scale then settling to 100%. Hold until 5.5s, then cut. Replace the example wording with my supplied exact phrase and align its landing to the emphasized voiceover word. Build the typography locally; preserve the underlying footage and audio. Check readability at mobile size.


2. Circular Typography
======================

Use: A boxing motif, recurring idea, product emblem, or short transition.
Build: SVG textPath with a controlled rotation in Remotion or a local compositor.
Check: Rotate the type, not the athlete. Keep a readable hold and avoid endless decorative spinning.

PROMPT
Create a 6-second circular-type graphic using “CONSISTENCY BUILDS CONFIDENCE • ” on an SVG text path. Use cream type, electric blue accents, and a dark background. Keep the central focal object upright and fixed. 0–0.8s: reveal the text arc clockwise. 0.8–2.2s: rotate the ring only 35 degrees with deceleration. 2.2–5.5s: hold the readable lockup. Finish on a clean cut. Fit the circle within 80% of the frame height. Keep all lettering in the local graphics layer. No repeated orbiting of the person or camera.


3. Crash Zoom
=============

Use: A reaction, a key word, or a quick change of emphasis during a speaking shot.
Build: Keyframed crop and blur on the picture layer only.
Check: Keep the face inside frame and the UI sharp. A digital crop cannot reveal new detail or reproduce optical perspective changes.

PROMPT
Using the supplied talking-head footage, add a motivated digital crash zoom on my marked word. From 4 frames before the word to 3 frames after it, scale the picture from 100% to 138%, keeping the eyes at the same screen position. Use a short directional/radial blur during acceleration, then settle sharp with no repeated bounce. Hold for 0.7 seconds and cut back wide at the next sentence boundary. Apply the transform only to the photographed layer; captions and YouTube UI stay sharp and fixed. Match the reference zoom curve when supplied. Preserve dialogue timing.


4. Whip Pan Transition
======================

Use: A fast handoff between related actions, angles, or product views.
Build: Matched slide transforms plus brief directional blur in the local edit.
Check: Match movement direction across the cut. Never leave two readable scenes blended together.

PROMPT
Connect supplied shots A and B with a fast leftward whip-pan transition. Over the final 5 frames of A, accelerate its picture to the left with horizontal motion blur. Cut at peak blur. Begin B offset from the right, moving left, and settle into its normal composition over 5 frames. Keep the total transition at 10 frames at 30fps. Maintain screen direction, do not mirror faces or logos, and keep editorial captions outside the moving picture group. Attach a short whoosh only if it supports the supplied music. Validate the cut frame and both adjoining clear frames.


5. Impact Frames
================

Use: A clearly visible boxing contact, a reveal, or an emphatic beat.
Build: One or two graphic frames, contact freeze and short settling displacement.
Check: Show contact before the effect. Use sparse low-intensity accents, not repeated full-screen strobes.

PROMPT
At the verified glove-to-bag contact in the supplied shot, add one dark graphic impact frame with a cream angular burst behind the glove. Freeze the contact pose for 2 frames at 30fps, then resume the action. Add a quick 6px picture displacement that decays to zero within 5 frames. Align one low impact sound to the contact and keep the dialogue clear. Build the effect in post; retain the original punch direction and anatomy. No looping rotation, repeated flashes, or new camera orbit. Use the reference contact frame as the anchor.


6. Match Cut
============

Use: Moving between a phone, a product, a speaker, and a related graphic.
Build: Frame selection, crop and an exact hard cut.
Check: Match position, scale and motion. The connection should read without a dissolve.

PROMPT
Find a clear frame in clip A and a frame in clip B with a similar focal shape. Crop both so that the shape center and apparent size match; preserve aspect ratios and avoid changing anatomy. Cut directly between them on the supplied beat or emphasized word. If the object moves, match its screen direction and phase of motion. Keep the outgoing shot readable for at least 0.4 seconds before the cut and the incoming one for at least 0.6 seconds. Do not morph or crossfade. Report the two chosen source timecodes and show the cut in continuous playback.


7. 2.5D Parallax
================

Use: A product reveal, illustrated explainer, or gentle editorial move.
Build: Foreground/background masks with different translation rates in Remotion or Blender.
Check: Fill newly exposed background and inspect mask edges. Parallax is not a full 3D orbit.

PROMPT
Turn my supplied still into a restrained 6-second 2.5D move. Separate the foreground subject, middle objects and background with clean masks. Create a subtle left-to-right camera impression: foreground travels 24px, middle layer 12px, background 4px in the opposite direction at 1080p. Ease into motion for 0.6s and settle over the last 0.8s. Fill only the background areas exposed by the move. Preserve the subject’s shape and text. Do not invent a rear view or rotate a flat cutout in 3D. Inspect hair, hands and mask edges at full size.


8. Mask Reveal
==============

Use: Chapter names, a named technique, or a product promise.
Build: Clipping masks and position keyframes; no generated text.
Check: No stray glyphs beyond the mask. Let the settled title stay long enough to read.

PROMPT
Reveal the supplied exact title through a fixed rectangular clipping mask. Use a left-aligned editorial layout, cream type and a muted blue rule on charcoal. Start the title 110% of its line height below the mask. Move it up to its final baseline over 0.45s with a fast start and gentle stop; introduce the subtitle 0.15s later with the same move. Hold the complete title for at least 2 seconds, then cut. Preserve letter spacing and line breaks throughout; do not fade individual glyphs or add random bouncing. Provide an editable local text layer.


9. Planar Screen Replacement
============================

Use: Replacing a filmed phone, laptop, tablet or monitor display.
Build: OpenCV tracking + Blender corner pin / masks; Remotion for the inserted UI.
Check: Check corners, occlusions, lens blur and motion at cuts. Keep photographed shake only once.

PROMPT
Replace only the display in my supplied phone footage with the supplied screen sequence. Track the screen plane independently within each shot and corner-pin the UI to it. Preserve the original phone body, hands, camera island, reflections and photographed shake with appropriate holdout masks. Match screen brightness and blur. Keep in-app character animation separate from camera movement and synchronize speaking states to the supplied dialogue. Do not interpolate tracking through cuts or add global shake to the finished composite. Compare source and result at the hardest motion and occlusion frames, then review every shot in playback.


10. Focus Pull
=============

Use: A revealed clue, a background reaction, or text that needs temporary attention.
Build: In-camera focus during generation, or separated depth layers in a local edit.
Check: A flat full-frame blur is not a focus pull. Keep the selected subject sharp and avoid haloing.

PROMPT
Create a deliberate focus pull using the supplied foreground and background layers. Hold focus on the foreground for 1.2 seconds while the background stays soft. At the marked story trigger, transfer focus to the background subject over 0.8 seconds, softening the foreground as the background resolves. Hold the new focus for 2 seconds. Preserve camera position, framing, text and subject geometry. Use depth masks and check for halos. If the supplied footage cannot be separated cleanly, explain that limitation and propose a simpler editorial emphasis rather than blurring the whole frame.


11. Beat-Synced Editing
======================

Use: An action montage, product reveal, or clear before-and-after audio demonstration.
Build: Waveform analysis, explicit markers, local cuts and music/dialogue mixing.
Check: Anchor to actual transients and story beats, not just equal time intervals. Music must leave room for speech.

PROMPT
Analyze my supplied dialogue and music. Mark the stressed words, musical transients and visible action contacts. Build a short edit in which the most important cut lands on one shared marker; use smaller beats for supporting cuts instead of cutting at every beat. Show a transcript strip, waveform and three aligned lanes for dialogue, picture and music as an explanatory overlay. Preserve complete spoken words and breaths, keep the dialogue foregrounded, and avoid clipping. Use only the supplied or properly licensed audio. Deliver the marker times and separate stems so the mix remains editable.


12. Branching Process Map
========================

Use: Explaining what the agent generates, builds and preserves.
Build: SVG connectors and timed cards in Remotion or an interactive graphic.
Check: Follow the narration order. Keep one active branch and do not present a dense finished tree immediately.

PROMPT
Build a 6-second process map on a warm neutral background. Top card: ASTRA. At 0.6s draw three connectors outward. Reveal child cards sequentially at 1.0s, 1.4s and 1.8s: GENERATE, BUILD, PRESERVE. Under them show small labels: face + voice; UI + captions; phone + original audio. Highlight only the branch currently named in the voiceover. Use thin dark connectors, restrained blue and orange accents, and ample spacing. Hold the completed map from 3s to 5.7s. Keep text editable and legible at phone size. No floating ornaments or endless orbiting nodes.
